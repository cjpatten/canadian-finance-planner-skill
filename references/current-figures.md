# Current Figures — Canadian financial facts (verified)

**This is the single source of current numbers for the skill.** Contribution limits, tax
brackets, benefit amounts, CPP/OAS/GIS rates, and other figures that change each year (or
quarter) live here, in one place, so they can be kept current and never conflict across the
reference files.

**How to use this file:**
- Any dollar amount, rate, limit, threshold, or bracket used in a plan or answer should come
  from this file. Use these figures verbatim.
- These figures OVERRIDE anything recalled from training. If a figure here conflicts with your
  memory, USE THE FIGURE HERE.
- For a figure NOT listed here, say clearly that it should be verified against the official
  source before use, and (when possible) verify it via web search against canada.ca / CRA /
  the Bank of Canada / the relevant provincial site.
- `[Source: URL]` markers are the official source. When a user asks for a citation, quote the
  URL exactly as written; never construct a URL. A line without a Source link has no citable
  URL on file — name the official page instead.

## Registered accounts (2026)
- RRSP limit 2026: **$33,810** (2025: $32,490). Deadline for the 2026 tax year: ~Mar 1, 2027.
- TFSA limit 2026: $7,000; cumulative room since 2009 (18+ since 2009): **$109,000**.
- FHSA: $8,000/yr, $40,000 lifetime, max $16,000 in one year with carry-forward. **There is NO minimum holding period before a qualifying withdrawal.**
- HBP withdrawal limit: $60,000 ($120,000/couple). Withdrawals 2022–2025: repayment starts in the 5th year (Budget 2024 grace); withdrawals 2026+: repayment starts the 2nd year, over 15 years.
- Lifetime capital gains exemption 2026: $1,275,000.
- Capital gains inclusion rate: 50% (the proposed 2/3 increase was CANCELLED in 2025).
- RRIF: convert RRSP by **Dec 31 of the year you turn 71**. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/rrsp-options-when-you-turn-71.html] Minimum factors: age 71 = 5.28%, **age 72 = 5.40%**, 20% applies at **age 95+**. Under 71: 1/(90−age); age 70 = 5.00%. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/t4rsp-t4rif-information-returns/payments/chart-prescribed-factors.html]

## Tax (2026)
- **Lowest federal rate is 14%** for 2026 (was 15%; the 2025 tax year is a blended 14.5%). The **Top-Up Tax Credit (line 34990) applies to the 2025 return ONLY** — it keeps 2025 non-refundable credits worth ~15%; it does NOT apply to 2026 returns. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-34990-top-up-tax-credit.html]
- Federal brackets 2026: 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/tax-rates-brackets/current-year.html]
- Federal BPA 2026: $16,452 (max, net income ≤ $181,440) / $14,829 (min, ≥ $258,482). Indexation factor 2026: 2.0%. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/frequently-asked-questions-individuals/adjustment-personal-income-tax-benefit-amounts.html]
- Alberta is NO LONGER flat-ish 10%: new **8% bracket on first $61,200 (2026)**, then 10% to $154,259, then 12/13/14/15%.
- Nova Scotia HST is **14%** since Apr 1, 2025 (NB/NL/PEI still 15%; ON 13%).
- Medical expense threshold: lesser of 3% of net income or **$2,890 (2026)** / $2,834 (2025). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/frequently-asked-questions-individuals/adjustment-personal-income-tax-benefit-amounts.html]
- Disability Tax Credit 2026: **$10,341** (up to ~$1,448 federal reduction); from 2026, 43 listed long-lasting conditions qualify with streamlined certification. [Source: https://budget.canada.ca/update-miseajour/2026/report-rapport/tm-mf-en.html]
- Home office **$2/day flat-rate method NO LONGER EXISTS** (2020–2022 only). Employees need the detailed method + T2200; self-employed use T2125 actual costs.
- CRA interest (Q3 2026, Jul–Sep): overdue tax **7%**; prescribed rate (family/spousal loans) **3%**; overpayment (paid to individuals) 5%. Rates are quarterly; Q4 announced late Sep. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/prescribed-interest-rates/2026-q3.html]
- Vehicle allowance 2026: $0.73 first 5,000 km / $0.67 after.
- Volunteer firefighter / search-and-rescue credit: $6,000 (doubled in 2024).
- First-time donor super credit: eliminated (2017) — does not exist.
- CRA is introducing **automatic tax filing** for low-income individuals starting with the 2025 tax year.

## CPP / OAS / GIS (2026)
- CPP 2026: YMPE **$74,600**; YAMPE (CPP2 second ceiling) **$85,000**. Employee 5.95% + CPP2 4%; self-employed total max **$9,292.90** (base $8,460.90 + CPP2 $832). CPP2 exists — do not omit it for self-employed users.
- **Quebec = QPP, not CPP.** Quebec residents contribute to the Quebec Pension Plan, which has its own (higher) contribution rates and maximums plus QPP2 between the YMPE and YAMPE. Do NOT apply the CPP self-employed max ($9,292.90) or a CPP monthly pension figure to a Quebec resident — verify current QPP rates, maximum contribution, and max pension at Retraite Québec (retraitequebec.gouv.qc.ca).
- **PROPOSED (pending Royal Assent — quote as proposed only): base CPP rate drops 9.9% → 9.5% Jan 1, 2027** (~$133/yr saving at $70K salary). The 2026 rate is unchanged. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/04/spring-economic-update-2026-key-measures.html]
- CPP max at 65 (2026): **$1,507.65/mo**; average new beneficiary **~$877/mo** (Apr 2026). PRB max ~$54.69/mo. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/payment-amounts.html]
- CPP timing: −0.6%/mo (−36% at 60) / +0.7%/mo (+42% at 70); retroactive start max 11 months (applications after 65). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/when-start.html] **CPP deferral does NOT increase the survivor's pension** (survivor benefit uses the contributor's unadjusted age-65 pension). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/cpp-survivor-pension.html]
- CPP death benefit: $2,500 base + up to $2,500 top-up (**$5,000 max**) since Jan 2025 when the deceased never collected CPP and left no surviving spouse. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/cpp-death-benefit.html]
- OAS (Q3 2026, Jul–Sep): **$751.97/mo** (65–74) / $827.17 (75+); max at 70 with full deferral **$1,022.68/mo**; indexed quarterly. Deferral +0.6%/mo to +36% at 70 (no early OAS; deferral also delays GIS). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/when-start.html]
- OAS clawback (2026 income year): starts at **$95,323**; fully clawed back ~$152,062 (65–74) / ~$157,923 (75+). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/frequently-asked-questions-individuals/adjustment-personal-income-tax-benefit-amounts.html] **Assessed on EACH individual's own net income, not household income** — check each spouse separately (a common error).
- GIS (Q3 2026): max single ~$1,123.17/mo; income cutoff single ~$22,800 / couple ~$30,096. **GIS is income-tested on income OTHER than OAS** (employer/survivor pensions, CPP/QPP, RRIF withdrawals, and interest all count) — a retiree with a meaningful pension usually exceeds the cutoff and gets $0. Compare non-OAS income to the cutoff before assuming any GIS.

## Housing (2026)
- **First-Time Home Buyers' GST Rebate** (Bill C-4, 2026): 100% GST rebate on NEW homes up to $1M (up to $50,000 back), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements on/after May 27, 2025.
- Stress test: higher of contract rate + 2% or 5.25% (unchanged). Since Nov 2024, straight lender switches at renewal (insured AND uninsured, same amount/amortization) need NO new stress test.
- Insured mortgage limits: CMHC allows **GDS 39% / TDS 44%** (32/40 is only a conservative guideline). Down payment: 5% to $500K, 10% on the $500K–$1.5M portion, 20% at $1.5M+.
- BC first-time-buyer property transfer tax exemption: full exemption to **$835,000** FMV (saves up to $8,000), partial to $860,000.
- GST New Housing Rebate (non-FTB): full 36% (max $6,300) up to $350K, phases out to $450K, zero above.
- Bank of Canada policy rate: **2.25%** (target; held at the June 10, 2026 announcement); Bank Rate 2.50%; prime **4.45%**. Next announcements: Jul 15, Sep 2, Oct 28, Dec 9, 2026. Verify current after each announcement. ## Debt (2026)
- **Payday loans: federal cap $14 per $100 advanced in designated provinces + criminal interest rate 35% APR since Jan 1, 2025.** Rates above 35% APR are illegal for personal loans (prior limit was 60% EAR ≈ 48% APR). [Source: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-46/section-347.html | Regulations: https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2024/2024-06-19/html/sor-dors114-eng.html]
- Payday loan definition: $1,500 or less, 62 days or less (~365% APR — warn users). [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/payday-loans.html]
- Debt limitation periods (how long creditors can sue): ON/AB/BC/SK/MB/NB/NS 2 years; QC 3; PEI 6. VERIFY BEFORE QUOTING — these are provincial statutes; name the province's limitations act instead of asserting confidently.
- Federal student loans: permanently interest-free (since Apr 1, 2023) — no interest strategy needed; attack other debt first. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/news/2023/03/government-of-canada-provides-interest-free-loans-for-students-effective-april1.html]
- **PROPOSED (pending Royal Assent): $10 cap on NSF bank fees** (Spring Economic Update 2026) — not yet enforceable. [Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/04/spring-economic-update-2026-key-measures.html]

## Cross-border (2026)
- US estate/gift exemption: **$15M USD per person, permanent from Jan 1, 2026** (OBBBA). Via treaty proration, Canadians with worldwide estates under ~$15M USD generally owe no US estate tax. Do NOT warn about a 2026 exemption drop — it was legislated away.
- FIRPTA on US property sales: 0% only if buyer-residence AND price ≤ $300K; **10% withheld $300K–$1M**; 15% above $1M.
- FBAR penalties (2026, inflation-adjusted): non-willful up to ~$16,536 per violation; willful greater of ~$165,353 or 50%. **FBAR/FATCA apply only to US persons** (citizens/green-card holders) — do NOT raise them for a Canadian snowbird with no US status.
- TFSAs are taxable to US persons; RESPs/RDSPs have Form 3520 relief (Rev. Proc. 2020-17).
- US-situs estate filing: a Canadian's US-situs assets (US real estate, US-domiciled stocks) over **$60,000 USD** trigger a **Form 706-NA** on death — a filing obligation even when the $15M exemption means no US estate tax is owed. Canadian-listed stocks and GICs are not US-situs.
- Substantial Presence Test (snowbirds): treated as a US resident for tax if present ≥31 days this year AND (days this year + 1/3 of last year + 1/6 of the year before) ≥ **183**. Escape via the **Closer Connection Exception** (actual days this year < 183, tax home + closer ties to Canada) by filing **Form 8840 every year by June 15**.

## Probate (2026)
- Ontario: first $50,000 EXEMPT, then $15 per $1,000 (1.5%) above ($500K estate → $6,750). The pre-2020 two-tier rate is dead. [Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/98e34]
- Manitoba: **$0 — probate fees eliminated** (Nov 6, 2020). [Source: https://www.manitobacourts.mb.ca/site/assets/files/1152/2020-11-06_notice_-_elimination_of_probate_charges.pdf]
- BC: $0 to $25K; $6/$1,000 on $25K–$50K (0.6%); $14/$1,000 above (1.4%). A ~$200 filing fee is commonly cited — verify before quoting. [Source: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/00_99004_01]
- Alberta: flat brackets, **max $525** (estates over $250K); $35 minimum. [Source: https://www.alberta.ca/court-fees]
- Saskatchewan: $200 flat + $7/$1,000 of estate assets. [Source: https://www.saskatchewan.ca/residents/births-deaths-marriages-and-divorces/dealing-with-death/administering-the-estate-of-someone-whos-died/when-the-deceased-does-not-have-last-will-and-testament]
- Quebec: **notarial wills need NO probate**; holograph/witnessed wills need court or notary validation (fee varies — do not quote a dollar amount). [Source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/justice-et-etat-civil/testament-succession/succession/settlement/succession-will/probating]
- Nova Scotia: bracketed probate tax, e.g. $1,002.65 + $16.95/$1,000 above $100K — schedule dates to 2015, verify before quoting.
- Alberta intestacy: if all children are shared with the surviving spouse, the **spouse inherits everything**.

## Estate — deemed disposition (structural, rarely changes)
- CRA treats all assets as sold at fair market value on the date of death.
- RRSPs/RRIFs: fully taxable (100%, not 50%) unless rolled to a surviving spouse or dependent.
- Capital gains: 50% inclusion on non-registered investments; principal residence exemption on the primary home.

## Durable fixtures (structural — verify only if a rule change is announced)
- CDIC coverage: $100,000 per deposit category per member institution (separate coverage for RRSP, TFSA, RRIF, FHSA, joint, one-name).
- Small business deduction: 9% federal on first $500,000 of active business income (CCPC).
- RESP: $50,000 lifetime per beneficiary; CESG 20% on first $2,500/yr = $500/yr, $7,200 lifetime cap.
- RDSP: $200,000 lifetime contribution; CDSG up to $3,500/yr, $70,000 lifetime; CDSB up to $1,000/yr, $20,000 lifetime.
